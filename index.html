Case Summary

The complainant, Ms. Priya Sharma, reported persistent cyber stalking activities spanning approximately 4 months (April–August 2026). The offender allegedly created multiple fake social media accounts to monitor the victim's online activities, sent threatening and harassing messages via email and messaging platforms, tracked the victim's real-time location using spyware installed on her mobile device, shared manipulated images of the victim on anonymous forums, and attempted unauthorized access to the victim's personal accounts. The digital forensic investigation has uncovered substantial evidence linking the suspect to the stalking activities through IP address correlation, device fingerprinting, metadata analysis, and behavioral pattern matching.

1. Executive Summary

This report presents the findings of a comprehensive digital forensic investigation into cyber stalking activities reported by the complainant (Ms. Priya Sharma) against the primary suspect (Mr. Arjun Kapoor). The investigation was conducted by the Cyber Crime Cell, Mumbai, under FIR No. 472/2026. Over the course of the investigation, 14 items of digital evidence were collected, 23 distinct digital traces were identified, and 9 incidents were documented spanning April–August 2026. The evidence strongly supports the complainant's allegations and provides a high-confidence identification of the suspect.

  • Forensic analysis of victim's mobile device (Android — Samsung Galaxy A54)
  • Forensic imaging and analysis of suspect's seized laptop (Dell Inspiron 15)
  • Analysis of suspect's seized mobile device (Samsung Galaxy S24)

  • Social media account investigation (Instagram, Facebook, anonymous forums)

4. Key Findings

4.1
Multi-Platform Harassment Campaign

The suspect orchestrated a coordinated harassment campaign across Instagram, Facebook, email (Protonmail), WhatsApp, and anonymous image forums using a combination of fake identities, VPN services, and prepaid SIM cards. Despite attempts at anonymization, operational security failures (OPSEC) resulted in multiple identifiable digital traces.

4.2
Spyware-Enabled Real-Time Surveillance

The commercial spyware "TrackMate Pro" was installed on the victim's device, likely through physical access during a social interaction (analysis shows installation occurred on May 28, 2026 at 14:23 IST). The spyware enabled GPS tracking, call/SMS interception, keylogging, and ambient audio recording. Data exfiltration was confirmed to a C2 server registered using the suspect's alternative identity.

4.3
Progressive Escalation Pattern

The stalking behavior followed a documented escalation trajectory: online monitoring → direct messaging → email threats → spyware installation → image manipulation → physical following → death threats. This escalation pattern is consistent with academic research on stalking behavior and represents a significant risk indicator for potential physical violence.

4.4
High-Confidence Suspect Identification

Through convergent analysis of IP addresses, device fingerprints, payment records, stylometric analysis, CCTV correlation, and ISP records, the investigation has identified the suspect with 91% confidence. The multi-source corroboration significantly reduces the possibility of misidentification.

5. Chain of Custody

All digital evidence was collected, preserved, and analyzed following standard forensic protocols. Chain of custody documentation is maintained for all 14 evidence items. Forensic images were created using write-blockers and verified with SHA-256 hash values. All evidence handling complies with the requirements of the Indian Evidence Act (Section 65B) for electronic evidence admissibility.

All forensic images are stored in tamper-proof encrypted containers with dual-auditor access controls.

6. Conclusions & Recommendations

Primary Conclusion

Digital evidence conclusively establishes a sustained cyber stalking campaign by the suspect against the victim over a period of approximately 4 months. The evidence meets the evidentiary threshold for prosecution under all cited sections of the IPC and IT Act.

Recommended Actions
  • Immediate arrest of suspect under non-bailable provisions
  • Protective order for the victim under Section 18 of PWDV Act
  • Seizure and forensic examination of suspect's remaining digital devices
  • Takedown requests to social media platforms for fake accounts
  • Referral to Victim Support Services for psychological assistance
  • Charge sheet filing with all 14 evidence items annexed as per Section 65B compliance
